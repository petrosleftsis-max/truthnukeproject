# Messengers of Truth - the narrative context

This file is the full narrative context of *Messengers of Truth*, a tactical RPG in development. It is for reading and thinking with: the world, the characters, the story pieces that exist so far, and how the game's mechanics and interface carry them.

**Sources:**

- the game's own conversations (all of them, word for word, in Appendix A);
- the characters' and enemies' descriptions;
- the clues, documents and other in-game text (Appendix B);
- the interface;
- what the designer has said about the story while building the game.

**How to read it:**

- Facts are kept apart from interpretation. Where something below is a reading rather than a stated fact, it says so.
- The story is unfinished. What exists is a set of pieces from different points of it: an opening, a middle stretch, and a scene from near the end.
- Some of it is placeholder or demo material, and that is marked.

**Content note:** one late scene (Alithia and her confessor) deals with a suicide attempt and its aftermath.

---

## 1. The world, as established

**The Dome:**

- The world lives under the **Dome**. Nobody, "even the eldest", remembers when it came to be.
- About thirty years ago the sky inside it "became pixelated": less beautiful, less detailed.
- The governments give no clear answer about any of this. "The Divine does a better job at it."

**The Divine** (referred to as "It"):

- It speaks through prophets.
- In the **Prophecy Miracle**, prophets across the whole Dome said the same words at the same moment. They gave identical answers to anyone who asked, wherever they were asked:
  > "The world is in danger. The Dome is there to protect you. But you will not need its protection forever. The Great Propagation will happen."
- Because the people trust the Divine, the governments cannot stop it, even when its guidance goes against them.

**The Hero:**

- The Divine has declared a **Hero**: "a young pure man of virtue, ready to lead his party under the direct guidance of the Divine".
- The opening happens on the day of his **Accolade**. The bells are ringing, and almost the whole Dome has come to watch.

**Idols and Idology:**

- Magic is the use of **Idols**. Not everyone has an Idol, and it is very hard to learn.
- Stories say **Yaldabaoth** "descended from the skies way before the Dome and bestowed upon us Their Gift". That Gift is Idols, the "Gift of Idolatry".
- A whole science grew from it, **Idology**. Cyrus jokes that people "used to think quantum physics are enough to make one go insane".
- **What Prometheus says about it:** no record inside the Dome points to Yaldabaoth's existence. Yet if "Yaldabaoth" names whoever gave the Gift, it definitely exists. And the parameters of Idols that use the Gate of Yaldabaoth differ a lot from the Gift itself.
- **Gates:** casters draw their power through **gates**, from weakest to strongest:
  - World, Hermes, Pandora, Charon, Tartarus, Nemesis, Sophia, Ogdoad, Gnosis, Yaldabaoth.
  - Outside fights there is also **Iris**, and **Mnemosyne** is planned.
  - Idol damage is the game's holy damage type.

**The Holy Order:**

- It has clergy, priests, monks and knights.
- Enfina could not be trusted to become one of its knights.
- Alithia, one of its most talented Idol users, has split its clergy with her questioning.
- Priests in the stealth stages ask strangers "which chapel do you serve?" (the chapel of the Morning Bell is one).

**The city:**

- It has city guards (Enfina is one), barracks, captains and a watch.
- The poor live in the sewers: "abandoned by society and family, whether by choice or not".

**The Wolf:**

- "It who hungers endlessly. It which corrupts everything in its path."
- It was known to exist, and known to be contained. It turns up loose in the sewers in the opening.
- In combat it is the first boss, **Erysichthon**, "The Hunger That Ate Itself" (see section 3).

**Cyrus's secret group** has "the intention to steal and weaken those who grow too strong".

**Factions:** enemies can belong to factions, which the party learns about through clues. The designer has kept which factions exist to themselves for now.

## 2. Names, numbers and symbols

**Truth and forgetting:**

- **Alithia** is *ἀλήθεια*, Greek for truth. The game is *Messengers of Truth*.
- Late in the story Alithia says: "This is the end. The messengers have already found the truth."
- **64**, the level cap, is the isopsephy (letter-sum) of ἀλήθεια. The character sheet's Levels page writes **ΑΛΗΘΕΙΑ** beside level 64.
- **55** is marked **ΛΗΘΗ**: Lethe, forgetting.
  - Every level past 55 stays hidden on that page until Chapter V begins. Then, with an effect, the hidden levels come out from under ΛΗΘΗ down to ΑΛΗΘΕΙΑ.
  - *Reading:* a-letheia is literally "un-forgetting", so the game reveals its last levels as truth emerging from forgetting.
- **The gate names** come from Greek myth and Gnosticism:
  - Hermes the messenger; Pandora; Charon, the ferryman of the dead; Tartarus; Nemesis.
  - Sophia, Wisdom, whose fall produces the demiurge in Gnostic myth.
  - The Ogdoad, the eighth heaven beyond the archons; Gnosis, knowledge.
  - Yaldabaoth, the demiurge.
  - Iris, the other messenger of the gods, is the gate for casting outside fights (Invisibility).
  - Mnemosyne, Memory and the mother of the Muses, is planned as the second outside gate.
  - A hack in the laboratory warns of turning a ward on its owners "without moving its sum, or the **Demiurge** notices".
- **Moira** (Fate) is, in the designer's words, "actually really important to the story and might end up more important than yalda[baoth] itself". Moira, and her mothers in myth Ananke and Nyx, are kept out of the gate names on purpose.
  - The late scene ends with Alithia casting a die: "The only object that can bring an ending to all of this. The die is cast."
- **Prometheus** is named for the Titan who brought fire and was punished for it: fire, a painful past, and a scholar of forbidden knowledge.
- **Erysichthon** is the king cursed with a hunger nothing could fill, who ended by eating himself. The boss is an undead wolf "grown to an abomination", whatever it swallows falling out through the hole in its belly.
  - The designer describes it as extremely gluttonous: "it always tries to devour everything, killing anything on sight, yet even if it eats itself, it can never be truly satisfied as its stomach has a hole from its own self hate and obsessive gluttony".
- **The story is in five chapters**, each leveling slower than the last. They end at levels 18, 33, 45, 55 (ΛΗΘΗ) and 64 (ΑΛΗΘΕΙΑ).

## 3. The cast

### The party

The bios are the game's own words; see Appendix B for them in full.

**Cyrus Rowe:**

- "A swift cunning cat with a bunch of tricks up his sleeves".
- The wanted poster in a guard room reads: "WANTED: a cat-eared thief in a red scarf. A good likeness, sadly."
- **His past:** his mother died, and he ran away from home. He has been stranded and surviving since his teens, which taught him "the skills and greed of a thief".
- **His scarf:** he keeps the red scarf that reminds him of his oath to himself: to become a hero.
- **His group:** those "childish ambitions" led him into the secret group that steals from and weakens the too-strong.
- **In the opening:** he has stolen a relic, and Enfina is chasing him for it. Yet he gives food to the sewer's homeless, and stops to help evacuate the people the Wolf threatens.
- **His voice:** quick and teasing ("Now, that'd just kill the fun, don't you think?").
- **In the late memory:** "A hero never backs down from saving one more person! ... If my demise can end all suffering. Then I... Why can I not do it...?!" Alithia answers: "You're a hero no matter what they say."
- **In play:** the rogue. The highest initiative and movement, Steal, Stealth, poisons and burning ammunition, items as a secondary action, and the stealth stages.

**Enfina Moonwish:**

- "A city guard who has trained and studied to perfectly respond to any combat situation."
- **Her past:** she was born into unfavourable circumstances. "Unable to be trusted to become a knight of the Holy Order, too useful to just leave her out of the picture." She is uncertain of herself, and trains relentlessly anyway, unrewarded.
- **Her heart:** "She does not raise her sword for herself. She raises it for everyone else."
- **Her voice:** terse ("You don't need to narrate it!"). Pragmatic about the law ("no law really protects the unlawful"). Gentle when it matters: "I'm sorry for only listening... I believe you can never be wrong no matter which answer you choose."
- **In play:** the wall. A greatsword, Guard, Grab and Hurl, climbing onto monsters, and a Measured Eye that studies whoever she hits.

**Prometheus Iliopoulos:**

- "A messy pragmatic scholar who can never be seen without a book and a drink."
- **His past:** a rising star in Idology from his late teens, he went quickly into high and important research. That is "now a painful memory that only destroys him further and further as time passes".
- **Now:** warm and caring, though he won't say it.
- **In the laboratory:** he recognises something in a barrel and won't talk about it ("I just remembered I need to restock on some drinks"). His workbench notes, mostly crossed out, have one line underlined three times: "The seal remembers how you hum, not who you are."
- **His drinking:** Alithia calls him "you damn alcoholic. You can't even move on from the past ... And yet... you found beauty in remembering."
- **About the Divine:** "No, there is no doubting it... This truly is the Divine. Their conclusions and reasonings... are so flawless."
- **In play:** the elemental caster and the party's hacker. He learns his spells from books, "rewrites the rules" of wards and seals in minigames, and can cancel enemy spells.
- **He is fragile on purpose.** The designer: "Prometheus is supposed to go down fairly easily if not careful."

**Alithia Undersen:**

- "A prideful and arrogant cleric with several capabilities of protecting her allies."
- **Her standing:** one of the most talented Idol users of the Holy Order, who "always kept the Divine close to her heart as It has guided her many times".
- **Her questioning:** she questions "even the smallest thing instead of showing silent obedience". Half the clergy think her blasphemous; the other half's faith grew stronger from her questioning.
- **In the late scene:**
  - She has seen something, "I saw the Divine", that she cannot tell anyone, being "forced onto an implied oath of silence".
  - She believes her selfishness may endanger the whole Dome.
  - She asks what it means to be divine.
  - She has tried to end her life. Her confessor stopped her.
  - Her confessor tells her that her one flaw is trying "to comprehend and see the entire future. And not even of just yourself. But of everyone."
- **Her voice:** sharp and sarcastic ("He really only understands caveman language"), principled ("This isn't the reason we are here!"), and fiercely loving.
- **Her neck:** her confessor asks after her neck, "I hope the fish scales are unharmed?"
- **In play:** the healer and caster of Idol (holy) and water. Her Intuition reads an enemy's coming turn. She also works illusions.
  - The designer's original note for her illusion path: "Having always been tricky, Alithia learns magic capable of drawing the attention of enemies away from her allies."

**The paths each can take at level 24:**

- **Enfina:**
  - Vanguard: "The charge."
  - Juggernaut: "The immovable."
  - Warden of Cover: "The protector. Enfina stands between her friends and harm."
- **Cyrus:**
  - Skirmisher: "The rapier."
  - Saboteur: "The ammunition."
  - Trapper: "Cyrus never fights a fight that is not already rigged."
- **Prometheus:**
  - Elemental Vanguard: "The blade."
  - Elemental Tactician: "The ward ... once, opening every gate at once."
  - Elemental Conductor: "The overcharge."
- **Alithia:**
  - Battle Chaplain: "Holy weapons."
  - Illusory Shepherd: "Protective illusions."
  - Inquisitor: "Reading them. Alithia sees what they will do, says so before everybody - and passes sentence on it, or simply forbids it."

### Others

- **Aleksander Asker:** Alithia's personal confessor, who loves her "as my daughter". He is a believer: "after whoever created the world, created love." His philosophy is "To live is to suffer ... It is because of love that mankind has continued to live. To suffer. And it is the very same thing that makes it all worth it." He turns her question back on her: "What does it mean for one to be Divine? Is seeing alone truly believing?"
- **The Clown:** "You do your best to bring peace and resolution into people's hearts! I do my best to bring peace and resolution into people's hearts! In the end, what is it that makes us so different, Cleric?" Alithia despises it, yet it is what makes her realise what she must do next.
- **The Hero:** the young man of virtue whose Accolade begins the story. He has not been seen yet.
- **Carl:** an old man of the sewers who tried to use his Idol from a book he stole off a scholar. It went wrong, and the scorch is still there.
- **Captain Vell:** in the clue demo, he skims ten crates of lamp oil a month out by the unwatched ditch. The quartermaster's ledger, a torn letter, the duty roster and his own seal prove it.
- **The Pit Champion and the sellswords:** they guard the laboratory, paid in advance. Their paymaster wrote in the margin not to expect any of them back. The Champion fights on anyway, for "the principle of it".
- **The stealth stages' people:** wardens, sentries, a captain, a hound, a watchful statue that sees through anything, a checkpoint priest, a night porter, an archivist, a cook, an aide.

### Enemies and bosses

**The enemies:**

- **Hired help:** sellswords, hired bows, the Pit Champion.
- **Fighters:** barbarians, rangers.
- **The Holy Order's priests**, and sorcerers.
- **Others:** an alchemist, a summoner with its bound beast, bombers, a mimic that copies spells, imps, ogres.
- **Creatures:** a stone golem ("words cut into it ... the words can be rewritten"), rats, and gnawers ("a sewer rat that died and kept eating").

**The bosses:**

- **Erysichthon,** "The Hunger That Ate Itself." It grabs, swallows and digests people, eats carrion and its own dead, and grows new heads and rib-maws as it is hurt. Its phases are "Gorge" and "The Hollow".
- **The Ash Warden,** "Warden of the Ash Gate": a giant in black iron, full of fire. Its phases are "The Ash Rises" and "Cinders and Fury".
- **The Bellkeeper,** "Keeper of the Silent Bells": small, quick, and deaf to everything but its own bells.

The Ash Warden and the Bellkeeper are combat demonstrations with placeholder fiction so far.

## 4. The story pieces that exist, in the order the title screen offers them

The title screen offers four ways in, one per character, plus the stealth stages, a clues demo, Arena Mode (fights to try) and a dungeon demo.

**1. Cyrus**, "an introduction to the world and a tutorial stage":

- The bells ring for the Accolade while Cyrus runs through the sewers, having lost a pursuer. His narration introduces the Dome, the Divine and the Prophecy Miracle.
- The sewers are too quiet. "It isn't just quiet. There is no echo."
- He finds the guards' looted supplies and the homeless beds, and leaves food. He remembers Carl's Idol accident and muses on Yaldabaoth and Idology.
- Enfina catches up with him ("Return the relic you stole!") while she is protecting the sewer residents.
- Mangled creatures appear, made of faces Cyrus recognises: "This isn't the Wolf's work. Whose is it then?!" He stays to help, and Enfina joins him.
- After the fight he urges the residents to evacuate. Then he sees what is coming: "It who hungers endlessly... The Wolf."

**2. Prometheus**, "narrative and gameplay team dynamics", in a laboratory:

- All four are together here. Alithia hears creatures ahead and asks whether to go on, and the party chooses.
- **The banter:**
  - Prometheus starts explaining Idology from a book, and Cyrus begs for "human language" ("... Is good book.").
  - Prometheus goes quiet at something in a barrel.
  - Cyrus and Prometheus joke about stripping the machinery to sell. Enfina agrees they could come back later; Alithia, scandalised, protests: "Aren't you a city guard?!"
- **The sellswords:** Cyrus finds the sellswords' contract and its grim margin note, with their uncollected advance beside it. That opens a parley with the Pit Champion: show him the contract, pay them off, bribe an archer, buy a sellsword's loyalty, keep him talking while Cyrus circles round, or fight.
- **Afterwards:** the party decides what to do with whoever was taken alive.
- **Prometheus's wards and seals:** his hacking puzzles live here (the Archive Ward, the Vault Terminal, the Humming Seal, the Binding Circle, and a run of harder seals), along with his own forgotten strongbox.

**3. Enfina**, "a difficult stage": the City Fight, a hard battle against every kind of enemy.

**4. Alithia**, "narrative snippet of mid to end game", in a church after "the reveal":

- Asker talks with Alithia through a door. (The whole conversation is in Appendix A.)
- She then walks the church, remembering each companion:
  - Enfina's listening;
  - Cyrus's heroism and despair;
  - Prometheus sharing a drink, and his certainty that "This truly is the Divine";
  - and the Clown.
- Until she has remembered them all, "a cloud" keeps her from leaving.
- **The finale:** "We've... lost too many. Our very goals have been shattered. This is the end. The messengers have already found the truth. And I'll be the one to finish it after all has been done."
  - Everyone is gathered in a small village. Alithia climbs the platform in its centre, while monks grow wary.
  - Her companions' remembered words sound around her: "I cannot refute any of these...", "I just want you all to be okay...", "You are so very petty, do you know that?"
  - She lets go of a die onto the boards: "The only object that can bring an ending to all of this. The die is cast."

**The stealth stages** are short missions:

- Mostly Cyrus: a vault, the watch, breaking Enfina out ("You came back for me." / "Where I hide, you hide."), and passing as a priest.
- Also a dark archive, poisoning an aide's supper and planting a forged letter, and a jumpy camp's checkpoint.

**The clues demo** is the Captain Vell investigation: pictures to search, clues to connect, and evidence to present.

## 5. How the gameplay carries the story

- **Alithia's flaw is her mechanic.** Asker says her flaw is trying "to comprehend and see the entire future". Her signature move, **Intuition**, reads exactly what an enemy will do next turn and holds it to that future.
  - When the party changes it, the marks shatter with a sound and a beat of frozen time, and "Future changed!" rises off the enemy.
  - The log says "The party managed to change the future that Alithia foresaw and the [enemy] is forced to adapt".
  - Alithia says one of: "I saw it coming." / "Not this time." / "The future bends." / "That is not how it ends."
  - Her Inquisitor path "passes sentence" on what she foresees, or forbids it.
  - *Reading:* fate (Moira), foresight, and changing what was foreseen run through her play and her story alike.
- **Truth and forgetting** in the level page, ΛΗΘΗ at 55 and ΑΛΗΘΕΙΑ at 64 (section 2).
- **Prometheus rewrites the rules.** His hacks are spells and wards shown as rules on a sheet ("Targets: Intruders", "Reach: 4"), which he edits. A badly balanced edit makes "the Demiurge" notice. He learns his spells from books, as a scholar would, while Alithia grows into hers as she levels.
- **Gates as cosmology.** Spending power climbs a ladder of myth from the World to Yaldabaoth. The Gift came from Yaldabaoth, and the deepest gate is his.
- **Coming back costs something.** Alithia's Resuscitate brings a fallen ally back at half health, but weaker for good: 10% less of everything but movement, stacking each time, and carried from fight to fight.
- **Cyrus is a thief in play too.** He steals enemies' items, sneaks, disguises himself as a priest, drugs suppers, and plants forged letters and stolen rings.
- **Enfina protects.** She guards, holds enemies in place, takes hits meant for allies, and (as a Warden) stands between her friends and harm, matching "she raises it for everyone else".
- **Enemies are people.** Fights rarely need to end in killing:
  - Enemies have nerve and can flee or surrender.
  - They can be bribed, talked down with leverage the party has found, or turned.
  - The fallen are taken alive, questioned, then let go or handed over.
  - The sellswords' story is built on this.
- **Illusions** (the Illusory Shepherd) put false cover and false allies on the field that only the enemy believes. An enemy's illusions fool the party in the same way.

## 6. The interface's words

- **Title:** *Messengers of Truth*. A panel of four portraits offers the four ways in, plus Arena Mode, the stealth stages, a Clues Demo and a Dungeon Demo.
- **The character sheet** has three pages: Sheet, Moves and Levels. Levels is grouped by chapter, "Chapter I ... levels 1 to 18". ΛΗΘΗ hides everything past 55 until Chapter V; ΑΛΗΘΕΙΑ sits at 64.
- **In combat:**
  - "Future changed!"
  - "Immune"
  - The turn queue's faces, cracked when a foreseen future breaks.
  - Boss entrances, with their name and title across the screen.
  - Stagger hints ("Feed it what it should not eat, and hit it hard").
- **After a fight:** "Search the fallen" and "Question the prisoners"; let go or hand over.
- **The journal:** clues, and an enemy codex filled in by studying enemies.
- **Gate shelves:** spells are grouped by gate. Each gate has a distinct first letter, because the interface names the gate a spell will be paid from by that letter.

## 7. What the designer has said about the story and its people

- **On Moira:** "Moira is actually really important to the story and might end up more important than yalda[baoth] itself."
- **On the gates:**
  - The third gate was renamed from Yaldabaoth to Pandora. Gnosis became the second strongest and Yaldabaoth the strongest.
  - Two more gates are for use outside fights: Iris (built) and Mnemosyne (to come).
- **On the levels:** "Have ΛΗΘΗ next to level 55 and hide the above levels. When the player starts chapter V, have an effect in the leveling page that reveals the higher levels. Have ΑΛΗΘΕΙΑ next to level 64."
- **On the chapters:** the game is to be five chapters, each leveling slower than the one before.
- **On Erysichthon:** "an undead wolf looking abomination that is extremely gluttonous. It always tries to devour everything, killing anything on sight, yet even if it eats itself, it can never be truly satisfied as its stomach has a hole from its own self hate and obsessive gluttony."
- **On Prometheus:** "Prometheus is supposed to go down fairly easily if not careful." Unlike Alithia, who learns her spells by leveling, he has to find spellbooks, and can only learn the spells that are his.
- **On Alithia:** her attacks deal Idol damage, and some ignore part of a target's defence, "so the others still have the highest damage outputs but Alithia is specifically better against tanky enemies". "Having always been tricky", she has illusions and confusion.
- **The paths' first concepts**, since replaced by the current twelve:
  - Cyrus: Swashbuckler, Gunner, Bombardier.
  - Prometheus: Spellsword, Demolitionist, Abjurer.
  - Alithia: Punisher ("inspired from relics of eld"), Carer ("caring for the wounded"), Illusionist.
- **On how fights should feel:**
  - No taunts. Attention is earned by placement, cover and holds.
  - Lingering harm should beat a single burst in the long run.
  - Late in the game, gates will be scarce across several fights between rests.
- **Factions** are the designer's to decide. They have not been named yet.

## 8. Open threads

These are questions the existing pieces raise but do not answer:

- **The Dome and the Divine:** what the Dome protects against, what the Great Propagation is, why the sky became pixelated thirty years ago, and what the Divine is.
- **What Alithia saw:** what "the reveal" is, and why Alithia believes her selfishness endangers the whole Dome.
- **The Wolf:** who contained it, and how it got out. Also, who made the mangled creatures in the sewers ("This isn't the Wolf's work").
- **The relic:** what Cyrus stole, and for his secret group.
- **Prometheus's past:** what his research was, and what is in the barrel at the laboratory.
- **The Hero:** who he is, and what becomes of his party.
- **Yaldabaoth and the Gift:** how Yaldabaoth relates to the Gift and to the Divine. Prometheus notes the Gate of Yaldabaoth's parameters differ from the Gift itself.
- **Moira, the die, and "The messengers have already found the truth":** what the final act is.
- **Who the messengers are,** and what truth they carry.
- **The Clown, and "peace and resolution in people's hearts".**

# Appendix A - Every conversation in the game, word for word

Written in the Dialogue Manager format. `Name: line` is a spoken line; a line with no name is narration. `- choice` lines are the player's choices. `[if ...]` is a condition. `do ...` lines are game actions: giving items, setting flags, starting fights. `=> ` jumps elsewhere. `[shake]` marks shaking text. They are grouped by where they happen. Developer examples (test, flag_example, walking_example) are left out.

## The stealth stages' briefings (title screen)

- **The Tools**: Get into the vault - the guard walking the corridor has the key. Throw pebbles to draw guards off, hide behind the crates, lift the key from his pocket, and listen for the guards you cannot see.

## The party

### Alithia

A prideful and arrogant cleric with several capabilities of protecting her allies.

Narrative: 
One of the most talented Idol Users of the Holy Order, she always kept the Divine close to her heart as It has guided her many times.

And yet the way people see her are very mixed, for many feel uncomfortable with how much she questions even the smallest thing instead of showing silent obedience. Throughout her studies and career she has infuriated a lot of clerics and yet has also helped the souls of many followers. Due to her debates, the clergy find themselves in an unspoken split between those who consider her blasphemous and those whose faith only became stronger with her masterful questioning.

Gameplay:
Even though she's really important for the party to not be worn out from the duration of combat, she is durable enough to survive hits herself. Prioritise keeping her in range of everyone!

She is also capable of weakening enemies to mitigate their offensive capabilities.

Said when a foreseen future is changed: "I saw it coming." / "Not this time." / "The future bends." / "That is not how it ends."

### Cyrus

A swift cunning cat with a bunch of tricks up his sleeves to hide from enemies and hunt down his targets.

Narrative:
An unfortunate circumstance which resulted in his mother's passing and him running away from home, has caused him to be stranded and struggling to survive since his teenage years. These harsh circumstances have forced him to develop the skills and greed of a thief.

And yet, despite the cruelty of reality, he still keeps on to his red scarf. The scarf that reminds him of the oath he swore to himself of becoming a hero.

These childish ambitions have lead him into a secret group, which has the intention to steal and weaken those who grow too strong.

Gameplay:
By having high initiative and movement, he gets to decide how a combat begins and where he is needed to go. He is capable of reaching even the most hidden enemy and hunt them down until they're no more. This makes him the only member who can get away from the team to take care of potent but frail targets, such as casters!

He also has the special capability of using consumables as a secondary action, making him the most adaptable member in the team.

### Enfina

A city guard who has trained and studied to perfectly respond to any combat situation.

Narrative:
Not born in favourable circumstances, she was faced with a lot of disadvantages. Unable to be trusted to become a knight of the Holy Order, too useful to just leave her out of the picture, yet still faced with uncertainty. An uncertainty that she also sees in herself and how cut out she truly is for the path she chose. 

And yet, she still raises her sword. She still trains unrelentlessly, to perform as well as possible, even if no one has any intention of recognising and rewarding her for it with a higher rank.

But Enfina's intentions are pure. For she does not raise her sword for herself. She raises it for everyone else.

Gameplay:
Capable of dealing the most damage and take the most hit, she is an invaluable piece that allows her to fend off the mightiest of foe. 

Her foes fear her reactive skill, so staying close to one will either force them to stay in place, or move and be damaged.

As a frontliner, she controls where the fight takes place. Make sure she is always in front of your allies and at the part of the map where you want the fight to take place.

### Prometheus

A messy pragmatic scholar who can never be seen without a book and a drink.

Narrative:
He was an up and rising star in Idology from his late teen years. This lead him to quickly get into high and important research. However... It is now a painful memory that only destroys him further and further as time passes.

Despite it all, or perhaps because of it all, he is now a really warm and caring person that, despite not saying it out loud, would always be there for you.

Gameplay:
The hardest unit to use well, he has the most potent capabilities in the party. Yet they are limited by resource and his frail frame makes him easy to be killed in but an instant. It is more important for him to stay alive, rather than cast spells earlier! Make sure to properly utilise cover!

He also can provide with utility, such as weakening enemies or teleporting allies. To make the most of him is to make the most of all!

## Hacks (Prometheus's wards and seals)

- **The Archive Ward**: It stuns whoever it calls an intruder, and wakes the captain. Turn it on its owners - stronger, and reaching further - without moving its sum, or the Demiurge notices.
- **The Binding Circle**: A circle cut into the floor, binding whoever it calls an intruder. Its lines pull on each other: every step drags others with it, every number stays between 0 and 9, and every step costs - even a step back. Turn it on its owners, stronger and further-reaching, without letting it come apart.
- **The Choir Lock**: A door that opens for a hymn sung right. Every voice you change pulls the others with it.
- **The Clockwork Door**: A vault door of gears and springs. Wind it open in the right order: every part turns others with it.
- **The Furnace Seal**: A seal that keeps the forge-spirit in. Cool it without choking it: the lines feed each other, and every step costs.
- **The Golem's Binding**: The words cut into its clay that say whose it is. Change who it answers to without leaving it unwilling to move or out of the habit of obeying - every line drags another, and every step costs.
- **The Mirror Ward**: A ward of mirrors that turns every glance back on whoever looks. Angle it away from yourself - each mirror tilts the next.
- **The Humming Seal**: A seal that opens for whoever hums at its pitch. Find it - amplitude, frequency, phase - and lock it in.
- **The Sluice Engine**: The engine that floods the lower vault. Drain it without blowing a valve - the pipes share their pressure.

- **Unpick the Spell**: Match the spell's hum before it lands, and it comes apart in their hands.
